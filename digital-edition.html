Digital Edition

This section presents the digital modelling of the edition, from textual selection and encoding strategy to the visualisation of variants and the synoptic comparison of the three witnesses.

About the Text

Gli Asolani by Pietro Bembo is transmitted in multiple redactions that are philologically significant and structurally distinct. The present project is based on Giorgio Dilemmi’s 1991 critical edition, which does not reduce the tradition to a single reconstructed text with one unified apparatus, but instead preserves the autonomy of the principal witnesses.

This editorial configuration has been retained in the digital environment. Rather than flattening the tradition into a single textual line, the project aims to preserve the individuality of manuscript Q, the 1505 edition, and the 1553 redaction, while making their relationships visible through digital tools.

Selection and Rationale

The mock-up was developed by selecting a substantial and representative portion of the text: pages 1–8 for witness Q, pages 1–4 for the 1505 edition, and pages 1–7 for the 1553 edition. The selected sample ends with the conclusion of the first poem.

This portion was chosen because it offers a particularly rich test case for digital encoding. It includes both prose and verse, narrative description and direct speech, and it displays a wide spectrum of textual phenomena: exact correspondences, expansions, omissions, substitutions, and structurally non-parallel passages.

It therefore provides an effective basis for testing both the encoding model and the interface design. In addition, for this portion only, the independent apparatus of M is available within the 1505 textual tradition, making the sample especially suitable for experimentation.

Markup Criteria

1. Editorial Model

The project preserves the tripartite editorial structure of Dilemmi’s edition. Instead of privileging a single witness, the digital model is designed to represent three autonomous textual forms, each with its own apparatus and its own philological value.

2. TEI Encoding Principles

The text has been encoded according to TEI P5 principles. The XML structure reflects both the formal articulation of the work and the critical logic of the printed edition, allowing textual units, variants, speakers, places, and editorial notes to be represented in a machine-readable and interpretable way.

3. Parallel Segmentation

A parallel segmentation model was adopted in order to align corresponding units across the three witnesses. This makes it possible to compare equivalent passages directly while also preserving non-parallel structures where exact correspondence is not possible.

4. Textual Units and Correspondence

The encoding operates at multiple levels. On the macro-level, the text is divided into narrative sequences. On the micro-level, paragraphs, verse groups, and smaller segments are linked through correspondence markers, allowing the interface to reconstruct aligned passages dynamically.

5. Apparatus and Variants

Variants are encoded through apparatus structures that preserve the internal logic of each witness. Additions, deletions, substitutions, overwritten forms, and editorial interventions are all retained, allowing the reader to move from a clean reading text to a more analytical philological view.

6. Diplomatic Fidelity and Editorial Intervention

No normalization has been applied. The transcription reproduces spelling, punctuation, capitalization, and typographical features exactly as they appear in the source edition. The only silent intervention is the recomposition of words divided by line breaks, introduced solely to preserve readability in the digital environment.

Critical Apparatus View

One branch of the project focuses on the visualisation of a critical reading text, based on the 1553 redaction, accompanied by the corresponding variants of Q and the 1505 edition. This view highlights the philological structure of the edition and offers a controlled representation of textual divergence within a traditional critical framework.

Synoptic View

The synoptic interface presents the three witnesses side by side, preserving their textual autonomy while making visible the relations encoded through parallel segmentation. It supports direct comparison of corresponding passages and reveals structural asymmetries where the witnesses diverge.
