Digital Edition

This section presents the digital modelling of the edition: TEI markup, parallel segmentation, EVT visualisation, diplomatic transcription, and critical apparatus.

Markup criteria

The Asolani Digital Edition project was developed as the final assignment for the Digital Scholarly Editing course. Its primary aim is to produce a digital version of Giorgio Dilemmi’s 1991 critical edition of Gli Asolani by Pietro Bembo. The source edition presents a number of methodological challenges. Rather than establishing a single critical text accompanied by a unified apparatus, Dilemmi chooses to present the three principal redactions separately, each with its own distinct critical apparatus. This editorial decision, while philologically rigorous, complicates the process of digital representation. The objective of this project has therefore been twofold: on the one hand, to preserve the original tripartite structure of the printed edition; on the other, to exploit the affordances of the digital medium in order to enable the simultaneous visualization and comparison of the three textual witnesses. To provide an effective and comprehensive representation of Dilemmi’s work, two main objectives were defined:

  • To represnte One text (1553 edition) allowing the visualization of the variants Q and 1505 throught a critical apparatus.
  • Tripartite visualization, enabling a complete and simultaneus presentation of the three texts, each accompanied by its own independent apparatus.
    • The encoding of the text has been carried out following the principles of TEI P5, adopting a parallel segmentation approach in order to allow a direct and systematic comparison among the three witnesses (Q, 1505, 1553). The segmentation operates on two levels: a macro-level, corresponding to the division into narrative sequences (
    ), and a micro-level, corresponding to the alignment of smaller textual units (paragraphs, sentences, and, where necessary, sub-sentential segments) within elements. Each groups the corresponding readings () of the witnesses, ensuring the closest possible semantic and structural alignment, even in cases of partial correspondence or expansion. Particular attention has been paid to maintaining a consistent alignment across witnesses, especially where one version presents additions or omissions, in which cases empty readings or non-parallel segments have been explicitly marked. The structure of the XML reflects both the logical articulation of the text and its philological stratification: the main text is organized into sequences, while paratextual elements (such as titles and the dedicatory letter) are encoded in dedicated divisions (e.g.
    ). No normalization has been applied: the transcription strictly reproduces the text as presented in Giorgio Dilemmi’s critical edition, preserving original spelling, punctuation, capitalization, and typographical features. All elements present in the source have been faithfully retained, including special symbols such as “|” and “·”. The only editorial intervention consists in the silent recomposition of words divided at line breaks in the source, in order to improve readability and ensure textual continuity within the digital environment.

    The mockup was developed by selecting a significant portion of the text. In particular, corresponding sections from each witness were identified: for Q, pages 1 to 8; for the 1505 edition, pages 1 to 4; and for the 1553 edition, pages 1 to 7. The sample was deliberately chosen to conclude with the end of the first poem. This excerpt was considered especially meaningful for several reasons. It includes both poetic and prose sections, features a variety of characters, and contains descriptive passages of settings. Moreover, it exemplifies a wide range of textual phenomena, including perfect correspondences among the witnesses as well as omissions, skips, and other variations. As such, it represents a particularly valuable test case for encoding, laying a solid foundation for further developments. Additionally, only for this specific portion is the independent apparatus of M available, as preserved within the 1505 edition.
