Section I

Bembo and the editorial programme

1.

Understanding the author

Pietro Bembo

Life
  • 1470–1547
  • Bembo was a Venetian scholar, poet, literary theorist, and cardinal.
  • He moved among major cultural centres such as Venice, Messina, Ferrara, Urbino, Rome, and Padua.
  • He played a decisive role in the early sixteenth-century Italian language question by proposing Petrarch as a model for poetry and Boccaccio for prose.
  • He sustained this linguistic vision through an editorial programme that made Trecento classics more widely available.

Section II

The work and its textual tradition

2.

Understanding the original work

Gli Asolani

The work

Gli Asolani is a treatise in three books, written in dialogue form between 1497 and 1502. It discusses the nature of love in a structure combining prose and verse (prosimetro) and framed by a narrative setting clearly modelled on Boccaccio’s Decameron.

The action unfolds over three days of celebrations in the gardens of the court of Caterina Cornaro, queen of Cyprus, at Asolo.

The content
  • Book I – Perottino: the unhappy lover speaks of the pain of love.
  • Book II – Gismondo: the happy lover illustrates the positive dimension of love.
  • Book III – Lavinello: he refutes the previous arguments and develops a Platonic theory of love, later turned toward an ascetic and transcendent vision of divine beauty.

Textual history

Princepes editiones

1505
  • Dedicated to Lucrezia Borgia.
1530
  • Removal of the dedication.
  • Rewritten according to the linguistic proposal of Prose della volgar lingua.
