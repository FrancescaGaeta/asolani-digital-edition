The author

Pietro Bembo

Life and cultural role

Pietro Bembo (1470–1547) was a Venetian scholar, poet, literary theorist, and cardinal. He moved among major cultural centres such as Venice, Messina, Ferrara, Urbino, Rome, and Padua. In the early sixteenth century, he played a decisive role in the so-called Italian language question by proposing Petrarch as the model for poetry and Boccaccio for prose. He then supported this linguistic proposal through an editorial programme, also associated with Aldo Manuzio, that helped make Trecento classics widely available.

The work

Gli Asolani

The work

Gli Asolani is a treatise in three books, written in dialogue form between 1497 and 1502. It discusses the nature of love in a text structured as a prosimetro, that is, a mixture of prose and verse. The work is framed by a narrative setting clearly modelled on Boccaccio’s Decameron: three days of celebrations in the gardens of the court of Caterina Cornaro, queen of Cyprus, at Asolo.

The content

  • Book I – Perottino: the unhappy lover speaks of the pain of love.
  • Book II – Gismondo: the happy lover presents the positive dimension of love.
  • Book III – Lavinello: he refutes the previous arguments and supports a Platonic theory of love, while also articulating a Christian ascetic movement toward divine beauty.

Philological basis

The critical edition

Cover placeholder for Giorgio Dilemmi’s critical edition.

Giorgio Dilemmi, 1991

Giorgio Dilemmi’s critical edition reconstructs the manuscript and editorial history of Gli Asolani. In the introduction, it discusses the historical and cultural context, the structure and content of the work, the different perspectives expressed by the characters, the author’s sources and literary influences, the compositional process, and the changes between editions, while also explaining the editorial criteria of the negative apparatus.

Main witnesses considered

  • Manuscript Querini Stampalia (Q)
  • 1505 edition (1)
  • 1553 edition (16)

The manuscripts

  • Codice Fondo Campori Appendice 280 (= γ.I.5.43), Modena, Biblioteca Estense

  • Codice Cl. VI.4 (= 1043), Venezia, Biblioteca Querini Stampalia (Q)
