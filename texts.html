1.

Understanding the author, Pietro Bembo.

THE AUTHOR

Pietro Bembo

Life
  • 1470–1547
  • Bembo was a Venetian scholar, poet, literary theorist and cardinal.
  • He moved between Venice, Messina, Ferrara, Urbino, Rome and Padua.
  • He played a decisive role in the sixteenth-century Italian language question by proposing Petrarch as a model for poetry and Boccaccio for prose.
  • He supported this idea through a precise editorial programme that made Trecento classics more accessible.

The work

Gli Asolani is a treatise in three books, written in dialogue form between 1497 and 1502. It discusses the nature of love in a prose-and-verse structure framed by a narrative setting clearly modelled on Boccaccio’s Decameron.

The action unfolds over three days of celebrations in the gardens of the court of Caterina Cornaro, queen of Cyprus, at Asolo.

Gli Asolani

The content
  • Book I – Perottino: the unhappy lover speaks about the pain of love.
  • Book II – Gismondo: the happy lover illustrates the positive dimension of love.
  • Book III – Lavinello: he refutes both previous arguments and develops a Platonic and then ascetic vision of love.

Princepes editiones

  • Dedicated to Lucrezia Borgia.
  • Removal of the dedication.
  • Rewritten following the linguistic proposal of the Prose della Volgare Lingua.
