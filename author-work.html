Author and Work

Section I

Author & Work

Pietro Bembo

Pietro Bembo (1470–1547) was one of the most influential intellectuals of the Italian Renaissance: a scholar, writer, literary theorist, and later a cardinal.

Deeply involved in the cultural life of his time, he moved among some of the most important centres of humanist activity, including Venice, Ferrara, Urbino, Rome and Padua.

Bembo played a decisive role in shaping the literary language of early modern Italy, most notably through his treatise Prose della volgar lingua (1525), in which he codified Petrarch as the model for poetry and Boccaccio for prose.

These proposals were not merely theoretical: they were supported by a coherent editorial programme aimed at making classical vernacular texts more widely available through print.

In close connection with the rise of the printing press, Bembo collaborated with leading printers and contributed to the dissemination, standardisation, and prestige of Italian literature.

The work

Gli Asolani is a treatise in three books, written in dialogue form between 1497 and 1502. It discusses the nature of love in a structure combining prose and verse, framed by a narrative setting clearly modelled on Boccaccio’s Decameron.

The work circulated in different textual forms and was later reshaped in print. It was first published in 1505, and subsequently revised and republished as Bembo progressively adapted his works to his linguistic programme.

Gli Asolani

The content

The action unfolds over three days of celebrations in the gardens of the court of Caterina Cornaro, queen of Cyprus, at Asolo.

  • Book I – Perottino: the unhappy lover speaks about the pain of love.
  • Book II – Gismondo: the happy lover illustrates the positive dimension of love.
  • Book III – Lavinello: he refutes both previous arguments and supports a Platonic theory of love, later turning toward an ascetic and transcendent vision of divine beauty.
Textual and Editorial History

A Work in Continuous Revision

Gli Asolani cannot be understood as a text fixed once and for all at the moment of its first publication. Its textual history extends from the manuscript phase to successive printed forms and reflects Bembo’s continuing revision of the work over several decades.

The manuscript tradition preserves evidence of the composition and rewriting process that preceded the 1505 princeps. The printed text was subsequently subjected to further revision, particularly in connection with the linguistic principles that Bembo progressively defined during his career.

The history of Gli Asolani is therefore characterised by a sequence of distinct but interconnected textual states. Their comparison makes it possible to observe the transformation of the work over time and provides the basis for both Giorgio Dilemmi’s critical reconstruction and the digital representation proposed in this project.

Section II

State of the Art

Editorial Scholarship

From Textual Tradition to Digital Representation

The study of Gli Asolani is closely connected with the reconstruction of its complex textual and editorial history. The coexistence of manuscript evidence and multiple printed redactions makes the work particularly significant for the study of authorial revision and the relationship between composition, publication, and linguistic reworking.

Within this tradition, Giorgio Dilemmi’s 1991 critical edition constitutes the principal philological reference for the present project. Rather than reducing the textual history to a single final text, Dilemmi reconstructs the successive stages through which the work developed and provides the documentary apparatus necessary to examine their relationships.

The Asolani Digital Edition does not aim to establish a new critical text. Its contribution lies instead in translating this stratified editorial model into a digital environment, where different textual states, variants and apparatus information can be explored through complementary forms of visualisation.

Section III

Dilemmi’s Critical Edition

Giorgio Dilemmi, 1991

A Genetic-Evolutionary Edition

Giorgio Dilemmi’s 1991 critical edition of Gli Asolani represents the philological foundation of this project. It reconstructs the complex textual history of the work through both manuscript evidence and the printed editions associated with Bembo’s continuing revision of the text.

Faced with this stratified tradition, Dilemmi does not seek merely to establish a single authoritative textual form. His edition is designed to document the different phases of the work’s development and to make the process of composition and revision visible.

The edition can therefore be described as a genetic-evolutionary model: its structure is intended to account for the successive stages of textual elaboration while preserving the evidence supplied by manuscript and printed witnesses.

To represent this development, Dilemmi gives autonomous textual status to three principal redactional stages: Q, the 1505 princeps, and the 1553 edition. Other witnesses are used to document the evolution of these textual states through appendices and critical apparatuses.

Editorial Model

Three Textual States, Different Apparatuses

The three texts shown above do not exhaust the textual tradition examined by Dilemmi. Rather, they correspond to the three stages to which his critical edition gives an autonomous textual presentation. The other witnesses are integrated according to the specific role they play in reconstructing the development of the work.

Q is an autograph manuscript characterised by an unfinished and stratified process of revision. Its apparatus therefore has a specifically genetic function. Dilemmi distinguishes a first, predominantly corrective layer — documenting immediate corrections, transcription errors and interventions connected with the material act of writing — from a second, evolutionary layer recording subsequent authorial revisions. Witness C is associated with this first textual state and is presented separately in an appendix.

The 1505 princeps, identified by Dilemmi as witness 1, constitutes the second autonomous textual state. Dilemmi presents it according to the author's final intention for that stage and uses the surviving readings of manuscript M to document earlier moments of its elaboration.

Finally, the 1553 edition, identified as witness 16, constitutes the third autonomous textual state. Its apparatus records the variants of witness 10, corresponding to the 1530 printed edition.

The result is therefore not a single text accompanied by one uniform apparatus, but an editorial structure designed to represent different moments in the evolution of Gli Asolani.

Editorial Criteria

Dilemmi’s Editorial Principles

In reproducing autograph manuscripts and printed editions that reflect the author's textual choices, Dilemmi adopts a principle of substantial respect for the documentary evidence. Editorial interventions, integrations and substitutions introduced directly into the text are marked by square brackets.

At the same time, the edition regularises selected graphic and typographic features in order to ensure readability and consistency. Common diacritics, word division and capitalization are normalised where appropriate, while graphic forms considered significant for Bembo's writing practices are preserved.

Particular attention is also devoted to punctuation. Because punctuation in the manuscripts and early printed editions is dense and multifunctional, Dilemmi intervenes when necessary to make the text intelligible while attempting to preserve its rhetorical organisation.
