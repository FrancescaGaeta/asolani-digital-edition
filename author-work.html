The author

The author: Pietro Bembo

The author: Pietro Bembo

Pietro Bembo (1470–1547) was one of the most influential intellectuals of the Italian Renaissance: a scholar, writer, literary theorist, and later a cardinal. Deeply involved in the cultural life of his time, he moved among some of the most important centres of humanist activity, including Venice, Ferrara, Urbino, Rome, and Padua. Bembo played a decisive role in shaping the literary language of early modern Italy, most notably through his treatise Prose della volgar lingua (1525), in which he codified Petrarch as the model for poetry and Boccaccio for prose. These proposals were not merely theoretical: they were supported by a coherent editorial programme aimed at making classical vernacular texts more widely available through print. In close connection with the rise of the printing press, Bembo collaborated with leading printers and contributed to the dissemination, standardisation, and prestige of Italian literature. His works reflect both the refinement of Renaissance court culture and a broader philological interest in language, style, and textual tradition.

The Work

The work

Gli Asolani is a treatise in three books, written in dialogue form between 1497 and 1502. It discusses the nature of love in a structure combining prose and verse, framed by a narrative setting clearly modelled on Boccaccio’s Decameron.

The action unfolds over three days of celebrations in the gardens of the court of Caterina Cornaro, queen of Cyprus, at Asolo.

Gli Asolani

The content
  • Book I – Perottino: the unhappy lover speaks about the pain of love.
  • Book II – Gismondo: the happy lover illustrates the positive dimension of love.
  • Book III – Lavinello: he refutes both previous arguments and supports a Platonic theory of love, later turning toward an ascetic and transcendent vision of divine beauty.
