Author and Work

Section I

Author & Work

Pietro Bembo

Pietro Bembo (1470–1547) was one of the most influential intellectuals of the Italian Renaissance: a scholar, writer, literary theorist, and later a cardinal.

Deeply involved in the cultural life of his time, he moved among some of the most important centres of humanist activity, including Venice, Ferrara, Urbino, Rome and Padua.

Bembo played a decisive role in shaping the literary language of early modern Italy, most notably through his treatise Prose della volgar lingua (1525), in which he codified Petrarch as the model for poetry and Boccaccio for prose.

These proposals were not merely theoretical: they were supported by a coherent editorial programme aimed at making classical vernacular texts more widely available through print.

In close connection with the rise of the printing press, Bembo collaborated with leading printers and contributed to the dissemination and standardisation of Italian literature.

The work

Gli Asolani is a treatise in three books, written in dialogue form between 1497 and 1502. It discusses the nature of love in a structure combining prose and verse, framed by a narrative setting clearly modelled on Boccaccio’s Decameron.

The work circulated in different textual forms and was later reshaped in print. It was first published in 1505, and subsequently revised and republished as Bembo progressively adapted his works to his linguistic programme.

Gli Asolani

The content

The action unfolds over three days of celebrations in the gardens of the court of Caterina Cornaro, queen of Cyprus, at Asolo.

  • Book I – Perottino: the unhappy lover speaks about the pain of love.
  • Book II – Gismondo: the happy lover illustrates the positive dimension of love.
  • Book III – Lavinello: he refutes both previous arguments and supports a Platonic theory of love, later turning toward an ascetic and transcendent vision of divine beauty.
Textual and Editorial History

A Work in Continuous Revision

Gli Asolani cannot be understood as a text fixed once and for all at the moment of its first publication. Its textual history extends from the manuscript phase to successive printed forms and reflects Bembo’s continuing revision of the work over several decades.

The manuscript tradition preserves important traces of the composition and rewriting process that preceded the 1505 princeps, while the later printed editions document further interventions on the text. These revisions concern not only individual readings, but mostly broader linguistic and stylistic choices, in connection with the principles that Bembo progressively codified during his career.

Section II

State of the Art

Textual and Editorial Tradition

The Documentary Tradition of Gli Asolani

The textual history of Gli Asolani is documented by both manuscript witnesses and a rich printed tradition. Together, these sources preserve different stages of the composition and revision of the work during the years.

Documentary Evidence

The Manuscript Tradition

The manuscript tradition of Gli Asolani survives through witnesses of different nature and extent, ranging from short autograph fragments to more substantial textual materials. They preserve traces of Bembo's writing and revision process before the print.

E

Codice Fondo Campori Appendice 280 (= γ.I.5.43)

Biblioteca Estense, Modena

A composite, partly autograph manuscript dated 28 October 1518. Fols. 13–142 contain the first two drafts of Gli Asolani. A note on fol. 142v, dated 23 October 1538, states that Federico Barbarigo copied the text from an original by Pietro Bembo.

C

Codice Chigiano L.VIII.304

Biblioteca Apostolica Vaticana, Rome

A composite sixteenth-century codex, partly autograph and consisting of 328 folios. Among its heterogeneous materials, fol. 231r preserves a short autograph fragment of Gli Asolani.

View digitised manuscript
M

Codice Marciano It. Cl. XI.25 (= 6671)

Biblioteca Nazionale Marciana, Venice

A composite sixteenth-century codex, partly autograph. Fols. 46r–51v preserve an autograph fragment of Book I of Gli Asolani, with marginal and interlinear authorial interventions.

Q

Codice Querini Stampalia Cl. VI.4 (= 1043)

Biblioteca Querini Stampalia, Venice

A sixteenth-century autograph manuscript with variants. Unlike the shorter fragments preserved in C and M, Q contains a substantial autograph portion of Gli Asolani, preserving numerous corrections and authorial revisions.

Printed Transmission

The Printed Tradition

The printed transmission of Gli Asolani is considerably broader than the surviving manuscript tradition. Among the many editions of the work, those of 1505, 1530 and 1553 represent particularly significant moments in the publication and revision history of the work.

1505

The Princeps

The first printed edition of Gli Asolani, published in 1505 and dedicated to Lucrezia Borgia.

1530

The 1530 Edition

A substantially revised edition of the work, published after the Prose della volgar lingua. The dedication to Lucrezia Borgia is removed and the text reflects Bembo's mature linguistic revision.

1553

The 1553 Edition

A posthumous edition, wanted by the author himself, published six years after Bembo's death. It represents a later printed state of the text, following the extensive revisions introduced into the work.

Section III

Dilemmi’s Critical Edition

Giorgio Dilemmi, 1991

A Genetic-Evolutionary Edition

Giorgio Dilemmi’s 1991 critical edition of Gli Asolani represents the philological foundation of this project. It reconstructs the complex textual history of the work through both manuscript evidence and the printed editions associated with Bembo’s continuing revision of the text.

Faced with this stratified tradition, Dilemmi does not seek merely to establish a single authoritative textual form. His edition is designed to document the different phases of the work’s development and to make the process of composition and revision visible.

The edition can therefore be described as a genetic-evolutionary model: its structure is intended to account for the successive stages of textual elaboration while preserving the evidence supplied by manuscript and printed witnesses.

To represent this development, Dilemmi gives autonomous textual status to three principal redactional stages: Q, the 1505 princeps, and the 1553 edition. Other witnesses are used to document the evolution of these textual states through appendices and critical apparatuses.

Editorial Model

Three Textual States, Different Apparatuses

The three texts shown above do not exhaust the textual tradition examined by Dilemmi. They correspond instead to the three stages to which his critical edition gives an autonomous textual presentation. The other witnesses are integrated according to the specific role they play in reconstructing the development of the work.

Q is an autograph manuscript characterised by an unfinished and stratified process of revision. Its apparatus therefore has a specifically genetic function. Dilemmi distinguishes a first corrective layer that documents immediate corrections, and transcription errors connected with the material act of writing; from a second and evolutionary layer recording subsequent authorial revisions. Witness C is associated with this first textual state and is presented separately in an appendix.

The 1505 princeps, identified by Dilemmi as witness 1, constitutes the second autonomous textual state. Dilemmi presents it according to the author's final intention for that stage and uses the surviving readings of manuscript M to document earlier moments of its elaboration.

Finally, the 1553 edition, identified as witness 16, constitutes the third autonomous textual state. The 1530 edition (witness 10) is not represented as a separate textual state because of its substantial proximity to the 1553 edition; its variants are instead recorded in the apparatus of witness 16. The 1553 edition was selected for autonomous representation as it reflects Bembo’s final authorial will and represents the final stage of the work.

The result is therefore not a single text accompanied by one uniform apparatus, but an editorial structure designed to represent different moments in the evolution of Gli Asolani.

Editorial Criteria

Dilemmi’s Editorial Principles

In reproducing autograph manuscripts and printed editions that reflect the author's textual choices, Dilemmi adopts a principle of substantial respect for the documentary evidence. Editorial interventions, integrations and substitutions introduced directly into the text are marked by square brackets.

At the same time, the edition regularises selected graphic and typographic features in order to ensure readability and consistency. Common diacritics, word division and capitalization are normalised where appropriate, while graphic forms considered significant for Bembo's writing practices are preserved.

Particular attention is also devoted to punctuation. Because punctuation in the manuscripts and early printed editions is dense and multifunctional, Dilemmi intervenes when necessary to make the text intelligible while attempting to preserve its rhetorical organisation.
